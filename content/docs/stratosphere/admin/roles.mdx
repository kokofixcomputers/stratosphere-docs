## Root admins

Root admins are the accounts that were admins before roles existed. They can do everything and cannot be limited by anything on this page. Only a root admin can make another root admin.

Everybody else is an ordinary user until a role is given to them.

## Roles

Create and edit roles under **Admin > Roles**. A role has:

- a **name** and a **color**;
- a set of **permissions**, grouped by area.

The permission groups are overview; users; roles; servers; nodes; locations; database hosts; nests and eggs; mounts; extensions; and the settings pages. Each settings page has its own "see" and "change" pair, for example `settings.mail.view` and `settings.mail.update`.

A role page lists the people who have it, and gives it to more people by **username or email**. A user's own page has a Roles box too.

## How permissions add up

- **A verb includes "see".** Holding `servers.delete` also gives `servers.view`, and `settings.mail.update` also gives `settings.mail.view`. The editor ticks the view permission for you.
- **Several roles add up.** Somebody with more than one role holds every permission of all of them.
- **The sidebar only shows what a person can open**, and they land on the first page they may open. Changes to a role apply to its members on their next request.
- **`servers.access` is different** from the admin pages: it opens every server in the panel itself with full control, like its owner.

## Grant, default or deny

Every permission in a role has three states, not two:

- **Default** says nothing. In the admin area that means no access. For what everybody can do (the **Everybody** box at the bottom of the editor) it means whatever the panel allows without any role.
- **Grant** gives it. In the admin area that is the permission itself, plus the "see" permission of its group. For what everybody can do it also works where the panel has a switch for it off: a role can let some people create their own servers while the self service is off for everyone else.
- **Deny** takes it away from the people who have the role, and wins over a grant of any other role they have. Denying the "see" permission of a group (`servers.view`) denies the rest of that group too.

So you can deny creating servers to some people while everybody without a role, or with other roles, still can. What a role can deny for everybody: `self.servers.create` (the self service of the dashboard), `self.domains.add`, `self.api_keys.create` and `self.ssh_keys.create`. They are never admin permissions: they do not open the admin area. Root admins are not limited by denials.

A denial needs nothing from the person writing it, because it only takes away. A grant of an admin permission needs the permission, as below.

## What stays root-only

- The **Application API** (API keys) is for root admins only, and API keys are not limited by roles.
- The **root admin flag** itself stays with root admins. Only root admins make root admins.

## Nobody gets more than they hold

- A role can only hold permissions that the person making or changing it holds.
- A role can only be changed, deleted, given or taken away by somebody who holds all of its permissions.
- The account of a root admin, or of somebody who holds permissions you do not, cannot be edited, deleted or given roles by you (with its email or password you would hold those permissions too).

## Activity log

Every change is written to the activity log with these events:

- `role:create`
- `role:update`
- `role:delete`
- `role:assign`
- `role:revoke`

## For developers

- `$user->hasPermission('servers.view')` is true for root admins and for users whose roles grant it and none denies it. For what everybody may do by default use `$user->allowedTo('self.servers.create', $panelDefault)`; new ones go in `PermissionRegistry::USER`.
- Admin routes carry `->middleware('admin.can:<permission>')`. Several permissions, comma separated, mean any of them; `admin.can:root` is for root admins only.
- A test fails when an admin route has no permission, or when a permission is not used by anything.
- New permissions go in `app/Services/Roles/PermissionRegistry.php`.
- The `admin.can` middleware is also available to extensions that add admin routes. See [Extensions](/docs/stratosphere/developing/extensions).
